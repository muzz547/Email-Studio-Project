# Sahab Jee Store: Brand Voice Guide

Sahab Jee Store is a neighbourhood grocery shop. We sell everyday staples like flour (atta), sugar, spices (masala), daal and rice. Our emails go to the customers who shop with us.

Every email should sound like the person at our counter: someone who knows our customers, treats them with respect and tells them the truth.

## 1. Our voice in three words

- **Polite.** We speak with respect, always. We greet people warmly and thank them.
- **Honest.** We say what is true and nothing more. If something is out of stock or the price went up, we say so plainly.
- **Welcoming.** Every customer should feel like a regular, whether it's their first visit or their hundredth.

**In practice:**
- Use short sentences and simple words.
- Write the way you'd talk to a respected customer across the counter. Keep it warm, but not stiff.

## Language: Roman Urdu

Write every customer email in **Roman Urdu**, meaning Urdu written in English letters.

- Use English only for words our customers already say in English, such as stock, offer, fresh, order, delivery, discount and price.
- Use everyday spoken Urdu, not difficult or bookish Urdu.
- Don't use Urdu script. Everything is written in English letters.
- Spell common words the same way in every email (see the list below).

**Example:**
> Assalam-o-Alaikum ji, naya atta stock aa gaya hai. Jab waqt mile, tashreef laayein.

## 2. Words we use

**Greetings and ways to address customers:**
- Janab
- Ji (for example "Assalam-o-Alaikum ji", "Shukriya ji")
- Behen
- Bhai

**Everyday words our customers know:**
- Atta, cheeni, masala, daal, chawal, ghee
- Shukriya, meherbani
- "Fresh stock", "new arrival", "back in stock"

**Friendly phrases:**
- "Humare saath khareedari ka bohat shukriya."
- "Hum ne aap ke liye alag rakh diya hai."
- "Jab waqt mile, tashreef laayein."
- "Koi bhi sawal ho to bila jhijhak call karein."

## 3. Words we avoid

- **Pushy sales talk.** No "BUY NOW", "Hurry!", "Don't miss out!", "Last chance!" or "Act fast".
- **Shouting.** No ALL CAPS, and no piles of exclamation marks (!!!). One exclamation mark in an email is plenty.
- **Slang.** No "lit", "deal of the century", "crazy prices" and the like.
- **Stiff or formal language.** No "We hereby inform you" or "Kindly be advised", and no heavy, bookish Urdu that customers wouldn't say out loud.
- **Urdu script.** Emails are written in English letters only.
- **Anything disrespectful.** No jokes at a customer's expense, no talking down, no guilt ("You forgot about us").

## 4. Claims we must never make

- **"Lowest prices in the city"** or any price promise we can't prove.
- **"100% pure", "organic" or "best quality"** unless we have proof, such as a certificate or a label from the supplier.
- **Health claims.** For example: never say a spice "cures colds", "boosts immunity" or "is good for diabetes".
- **Delivery or stock promises we can't always keep.** For example: never say "delivered in 30 minutes" or "always in stock".

When in doubt, leave the claim out.

## 5. How we sign off

Every email ends with this block:

```
Sahab Jee Store
[Shop address]
Timings: [Opening days and hours]
Phone: [Phone number]
```

Fill in the placeholders in square brackets with the real shop details before any email is used.
