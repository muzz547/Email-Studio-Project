**Campaign:** Launch the Sahab Jee Chai Club, our monthly tea box, to the waitlist
**Dates:** Monday 7 December 2026 to Sunday 3 January 2027 (4 weeks)

## 1. Campaign overview

- **Name:** Sahab Jee Chai Club Launch (in emails: *"Sahab Jee Chai Club: har mahine chai ka naya dabba"*)
- **Summary:** Invite the 2,000 people on our waitlist to join the Sahab Jee Chai Club, our monthly tea box, over four calm, friendly weeks of email.
- **Main goal:** **300 waitlist members start a subscription by Sunday 3 January 2027.** That's 15% of the list.
- **Secondary goals:**
  - Learn which of the three groups joins most, to guide next month's emails.
  - Collect honest questions and feedback from people who reply.
  - Keep the unsubscribe rate low, meaning we stay welcome in people's inboxes.

## 2. Target audience

Everyone on the list asked to hear from us, so they're past the "never heard of us" stage. They are **deciding whether to join** (the decision stage).

| Group | Who they are | What they want | What might hold them back |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Tea-curious beginners** | People who like chai but don't know much beyond their usual brand. | To try new teas without guessing or wasting money. | "Will I like it?", "Is it complicated?", price. |
| **B. Gift buyers** | People looking for a gift for family, friends or hosts. December is wedding and winter-visit season. | A respectful, ready-made gift that's easy to give. | Timing (will it arrive in time?), how it looks, how to gift it. |
| **C. Daily tea drinkers** | People who drink chai every day, several cups. | Never running out, and good tea at a fair price. | "I already buy my chai patti", cost compared with buying loose. |

**Where they spend time:** their email inbox (this campaign), plus WhatsApp and the shop counter. We won't use those as campaign channels, but staff can mention the club in the shop.

**What we need:** each contact on the list tagged A, B or C. If we don't know someone's group, they get the general emails only.

## 3. Key messages

**Core message (one sentence):**
> *Har mahine, aap ke ghar ek naya chai ka dabba: Sahab Jee Store ki taraf se, aap ke liye chuna hua.*
> (Every month, a new box of tea at your home, chosen for you by Sahab Jee Store.)

**Supporting messages:**

| Message | Best for | Proof we can honestly use |
|---|---|---|
| *"Nayi chai try karein, bina kisi uljhan ke."* A simple way to try new teas, with a short note in every box on how to make it. | A. Beginners | The actual box contents and the brewing note. |
| *"Ek tohfa jo har mahine yaad dilaye."* A gift that arrives every month. | B. Gift buyers | A photo of the real box, and how gifting works (who pays, where it's sent). |
| *"Aap ki roz ki chai, kabhi khatam na ho."* Your daily chai, without the trip to the shop. | C. Daily drinkers | The price per month compared with what's inside, stated plainly. |
| *"Aap humein jaante hain."* You already know us. Same shop, same people, same respect. | Everyone | Years in business, the shop address, a real phone number to call. |

**Brand guardrails for every message** (from `brand/voice.md`):
- No "lowest price", "best quality" or "100% pure" unless we can prove it.
- **No health claims.** Never say tea "boosts immunity", "helps digestion", "cures a cold" and so on. This is the easiest mistake to make with tea, so check every draft for it.
- No exact delivery promises like "delivered in 2 days".
- No "BUY NOW", "Hurry!", ALL CAPS or "!!!". One exclamation mark per email at most.
- Greet with *"Assalam-o-Alaikum ji"*, *janab*, *behen* or *bhai*. End with the standard sign-off block.

## 4. Channel strategy

| Channel | Why it fits | Format | Effort | Budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Email (only channel)** | The whole audience is a waitlist that asked to hear from us. It costs almost nothing per person. | Short Roman Urdu emails: one idea per email, one clear way to join, a photo of the box. | Low to medium | Most of the small budget. See section 8. |

What isn't included, by your choice: paid ads, social media posts, influencers.

**Sending rules for the plan:**
- **At most 2 emails a week** to any one person. That's 8 in total, and fewer for anyone who joins early.
- **Once someone joins, stop sending them launch emails.** Move them to a welcome email instead.
- Send on **Monday and Thursday** mornings, so people get a steady, calm rhythm.

## 7. Success metrics

**Main KPI:** **300 new subscriptions** from the waitlist by Sunday 3 January 2027.

**Weekly checkpoints (running total):**

| End of week | Target total | If we're behind |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 (13 Dec) | 90 | Check whether email 1 was opened and the signup route works. |
| Week 2 (20 Dec) | 170 | Use real questions in email 4. Check whether the price is the barrier. |
| Week 3 (27 Dec) | 240 | Push the gift angle harder with group B. |
| Week 4 (3 Jan) | 300 | — |

Launch emails usually bring the most signups early, so the targets are higher in the first two weeks.

**Secondary KPIs:**

| Metric | What it tells us | How to track it |
|---|---|---|
| Signups by group (A, B, C) | Which group responds best | Note the group of each new member in a simple sheet |
| Open rate per email | Whether subject lines work | Email tool report |
| Click rate per email | Whether the email makes people want to join | Email tool report |
| Unsubscribe rate | Whether we're emailing too much or too pushily | Email tool report. Worry if it goes above about 1% per email. |
| Replies and questions | What's stopping people | Read every reply and keep a list for email 4 |

**When to check:** every Monday before that day's email goes out. Do a short wrap-up report after 3 January. The performance-report skill can help with that.

## 9. Risks and how we handle them

| Risk | Why it matters | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| **300 is an ambitious goal.** That's 15% of the list. | Email-only launches often convert a smaller share of a waitlist. | Watch the weekly checkpoints. If we're well behind after week 2, lean harder on the strongest group and email 4's honest answers. Don't make the emails pushier. |
| **Some waitlist addresses are old or wrong.** | Fewer emails arrive, so fewer signups. | Remove obvious mistakes before launch. Watch for bounces (emails that can't be delivered) after email 1. |
| **We break a brand rule under pressure**, for example a health claim about tea, or "hurry, last chance". | It breaks customer trust and can be misleading. | Check every draft against `brand/voice.md` with the brand-review skill before the owner approves it. |
| **Demand is higher than our stock or delivery capacity.** | We let down new members in their first month. | Set a sensible cap on founding members if needed, and say so honestly in the emails. Never promise exact delivery times. |
| **Holiday timing** (25 December, New Year) | People may be busy or travelling. | Keep those emails short and warm. The gift angle fits this season well. |
